Satellites and radars are tools we use to “see” things that are far away, even up in the sky or across long distances.

Satellites are machines that we send into space. They orbit (circle) around the Earth, kind of like how the Moon does. They have cameras and sensors that can take pictures, measure weather, or send signals for things like TV, GPS, and the internet. They work by collecting information and sending it back down to Earth using radio waves.

Radar stands for “Radio Detection and Ranging.” It’s a system that helps us find objects and see how far away they are. A radar machine sends out radio waves, which bounce off objects like airplanes, ships, or even rain clouds. When the waves bounce back, the radar uses them to figure out where the object is and how fast it’s moving.

In simple terms: Satellites watch and send information from space. Radar sends out signals and listens for echoes to find things.
